Polyominoes that pack better tilted than grid-aligned

Each piece is a discretised keyed rhombus. It packs the plane in a rhombille arrangement — three orientations 120° apart, which no combination of integer translations and 90° rotations can reproduce — more densely than the best grid-aligned packing anyone has found for it. The comparison reduces to area per copy: the cell count cancels, so all that matters is D_aligned / D_tilted > 1. That is why the pieces can be hollowed so aggressively, and why cell count is a nearly free axis to minimise.

How the aligned density is established. "Checked to m copies/period" means every grid-aligned packing that repeats with at most m copies per period has been enumerated and shown infeasible — equivalently, every m-copy lattice packing, in all 8 orientations, over every period shape, shear and aspect ratio.

Two independent implementations agree on the 1-copy figure: a fast search over reduced lattice bases, and a deliberately naive enumeration of period lattices in canonical form that shares no code with it. The 2-copy search covers two copies in the same orientation as well as in different ones. Levels beyond 2 come from exact max-packing on the torus with a SAT solver — for the 27-cell piece that was 3,565 period lattices at m=2 and 7,411 at m=3, all infeasible.

Not covered: anything beyond the per-entry "checked to" figure above, and aperiodic packings. Aligned densities are therefore best-known, not proven optimal. A level counts only when no torus in it timed out — a level that finds no packing but leaves some undecided proves nothing, and is not counted.

Which way these bounds point. Every cell count here is an upper bound on the smallest possible polyomino: it says a piece that small exists. Nothing here shows a smaller one is impossible — no lower bound on the minimum is known.

Rendering. Every panel is a pixel-exact raster, one array entry per grid cell, nearest neighbour, with nearby copies forced to different colours. Each aligned panel is asserted to have zero overlapping cells and a painted density matching the claimed one — a stale orientation index once drew a genuinely invalid packing, with copies overlapping in some places and leaving real gaps in others.
